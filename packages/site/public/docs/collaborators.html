Collaborators and visibility live

Invite people by handle with a role, make a repository public or private, and see every session and token that acted on it.

Roles

Role May
read Clone, fetch, view.
write Also push beans.
maintain Also answer decision cards, manage deploy tokens, change protected paths.
owner Everything, settings and deletion included. Not invitable.

Invitations wait 14 days and appear on the invitee's Home. Settings → Collaborators changes or removes roles, and an access log records every invitation, answer, change and removal.

Visibility

  • Private: only the owner and people they invite. Anyone else gets a 404, on every transport.
  • Public: anyone reads and clones, signed in or not. Pushing still needs a role.

One rule, every transport

Access is decided in one place for HTTPS git, SSH git, MCP and the web. Tokens, keys and agent sessions get their person's role capped by their scopes. Answering decision cards, managing deploy tokens and changing settings are done only by a person on the web, never by a token. The rule is tested as a matrix of every role, visibility and transport: 128 cases.

The People tab

Who has access, and every credential that cloned or pushed: the person it acted for, its name (laptop git, Claude Code, a deploy token), last read, last push. A collaborator can leave from here.

Organizations rolling out

An organization owns repositories together. Its handle shares one namespace with people's handles, so <beanstalk host>/<handle> is a person or an organization, never both. Create one from the menu under your handle; you become its owner. Invite people by handle; they accept or decline on their Home.

  • Owner: everything, including other owners and deleting the organization. The last owner cannot leave.
  • Admin: settings, secrets and members, and every repository as its owner.
  • Member: the organization's base permission (none, read or write) on every repository; creates repositories unless only admins may.
  • Viewer: the base permission, at most read.

A collaborator role on one repository adds to the base permission; the stronger one counts. A private repository of an organization is not found for anyone outside it. You can create a repository under an organization from New repository, and move one of yours into an organization you administer (or back) from its Settings. The same one rule decides access over HTTPS, SSH, MCP and the web; its test matrix adds 354 cases of organization role, base permission, collaborator role, visibility and transport.

Teams and email invitations are coming soon.

How a bean grows.

A bean grows into a sprout, and sprouts mature into the stalk together.

Bean
An agent's or a human's idea, queued or being written.
Sprout
Once a bean passes its tests on a soft fork, it blooms into a sprout, and others get its updates instantly.
Stalk
Beanstalk promotes the sprouts queued since the last check, together, in one batch. Once they pass together, they all join the stalk.

Sprouts are tested alone. The stalk is tested together.

Twelve agents land at once. Each sprout passed on its own, but two can still clash, so the stalk tests them together, and pulls out only the culprit if they don't. When one stalk check finishes, the next starts at once on whatever landed meanwhile.

Merge queues make green work wait. Beanstalk shares it instantly.

Checks in parallel are table stakes. What matters is what happens after a check passes, and after one fails.

In our races, Beanstalk finishes 1.6–2.3× sooner than a GitHub-style merge queue (12 agents, 40 colliding tasks, three runs).

Ask the repo anything.

Type a question in plain words and the page reshapes around the answer: the files, the beans that touched them, the diffs and who's working there now. Your agents can ask the same questions before they edit.

Jev

It answers fast because a small model picks the view instead of generating it. Jev is a decision model: it picks what to show in about half a second instead of writing a page. Live pages still take a few seconds to fill in today.

When work collides, it sorts itself out.

Most clashes between agents are small. Beanstalk handles those, and saves people for the ones that matter.

Broken work goes back to its author

A red check sends the bean back to the agent that wrote it, with the failing tests and what changed underneath it. The stalk never sees it.

Stale test values reconcile themselves

When two beans clash only because a test still expects an old value, Beanstalk updates it and moves on.
